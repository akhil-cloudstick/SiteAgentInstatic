## 3. Option 2 is built

You asked us to price it before defaulting to normalising. Here is the price: **much
lower than either of us assumed**, so we built it instead of quoting it.

### Why it was cheap

The artefact writer **already** understood directory URLs — `/about-us/` has always
mapped to `about-us/index.html`. Nothing ever asked for that shape. The publish step
computed `/about-us` and never `/about-us/`, so every site published flat regardless
of the URLs it arrived with.

So this was not a new capability. It was a decision that had never been made explicit.

### What changed

- **`settings.trailingSlash`** — a per-site setting, **off by default**, so no
  existing site's URLs move.
- **One decision point** (`applyRoutePolicy`) used by both bake paths — pages and
  collection entries — so the bake, the sitemap and the reader cannot drift into
  disagreeing about a site's URL shape.
- **The sitemap follows the site.** A directory-baked site lists `/about-us/`.
  Emitting the flat form there would have put the URL the host redirects *away from*
  into the sitemap of every page.
- **The artefact reader accepts both shapes.** An old inbound link or a host's own
  redirect asking for `/about-us` on a directory-baked site still hits the fast path
  instead of forcing a full re-render of a page already on disk.

### What it means for your numbers

All three of your affected sets keep their URLs:

| Artefact | Count | After |
|---|---|---|
| Live canonicals | 496 | unchanged, and now pointing at the URL actually served |
| Legacy 301 map | 190 rules | targets resolve directly, no 308 hop |
| Blog 301 map | 189 rules | same |

No redirect chains. The ~244 non-post pages keep their addresses exactly. The 189 blog
URLs still move, but that is your restructure, not our URL shape — which was your
whole point about not changing everything at once.

### How you turn it on — no request to us

The setting rides in the site shell your bundle already carries. Ship:

```json
"settings": { "trailingSlash": true }
```

and `import_replace` applies it. The decision lives in your bundle rather than in a
ticket to us, which also means you can prove it in a preview before committing.

Both conventions stay valid — a site that never used trailing slashes leaves it off.
That is why it is a site setting and not a platform default: flipping it globally
would have rewritten the URL of every page already published, which is precisely the
harm you were arguing against.

### On the last two

**`export_bundle` returning bytes** — you offered to accept this and we should have
built it when you first raised it. Your sequencing step 0 is now genuinely runnable,
**including media**, rather than via the manifest workaround.

Your §4 question about the 6 MB limit was the right one to ask, and the answer was
that our limit was wrong. We measured rather than estimated: `akhil`'s 398 pages are
25.3 MB raw and **5.18 MB zipped** — page trees compress at under 5x, not the 10x we
had assumed. Scaled to your 497 pages that is **~6.5 MB**, over the cap. Your
content-only snapshot would have fallen back to an unreachable path at exactly the
moment it mattered.

So we removed the ceiling rather than raising it — a bigger number only moves the
cliff one site further out. The archive now splits across parts: request
`part=1..parts`, follow `nextPart`, concatenate the decoded bytes in order, and check
the returned **`sha256` of the whole archive** before trusting it as rollback
material. Small archives still arrive in a single response exactly as before.

**One correction, and your B0.6a is what found it.** Writing that test back to
ourselves made us re-check the first cut of chunking, and it was broken. A zip embeds
timestamps, so two exports of an unchanged site differ byte-for-byte — measured on
`client-b`, which hashed `070105ee…` and `44f736ba…` across consecutive runs at an
identical 2,053,204 bytes. Our first version re-exported on every part request, so
parts came from different archives and could never have reassembled. B0.6a would have
failed on its first run.

Fixed: the first call now returns an **`exportId`**, and parts 2..n pass it back to be
served from the archive already on disk. Verified against the live connector — the
same `exportId` re-read returns
`d7c64c60a19400ff9c0ce9632589928861fbe6920c0d95dba22cd427830865c9` on both calls, and
a traversal attempt on `exportId` is refused. **Pass `exportId` for every part after
the first**; omitting it starts a fresh export and the parts will not fit together.

**Answering your other two questions:** a site from `connector_create_site` arrives
with exactly the four system tables (`pages`, `posts`, `components`, `layouts`), no
collections and no rows — the same shape as `akhil`, so your B0.2 assumption holds.
And we will tell you when things land rather than have you poll.

**On B0.6b** — keep it, and it is the right instinct. The hash is taken over the whole
